Here's a summary of what the implementation does and why it's novel compared to the existing `Betweenness-Centrality_Bottleneck_Alignment.py`:

---

**Existing approach** (already in codebase): unweighted BC on QIG → rank sort → zip.

**BCBDRM novelties:**

**1. Interaction-Frequency Weighted BC on QIG**
Edge `(q1, q2)` carries weight = number of 2-qubit gates between them. During Brandes' traversal, `sigma[w] += sigma[v] * edge_weight(v, w)`, so qubits bridging *heavily-used* paths accumulate more centrality than topologically-equivalent qubits on lighter edges.

**2. Dual Rank with degree tiebreaker**
Sort key is `(BC, degree)` for both logical and physical — when two qubits have equal BC, higher-degree ones rank higher, matching hardware hubs to circuit hubs.

**3. Two-Phase Bridge-to-Bridge Placement**
- **Phase 1 (Bridge Seeding):** Logical qubits with `BC > 0` (true structural bridges) are matched rank-to-rank against physical bridges. This anchors the mediation skeleton.
- **Phase 2 (Neighbor-Aware Fill):** Logical qubits with `BC == 0` (graph leaves/isolates) are placed on the *closest available physical qubit* to their already-placed QIG neighbors, minimising distance for pairs that interact but aren't bridges — without any exponential search.

**Complexity:** O(V·E) for Brandes' + O(n²) for Phase 2 fill → well within polynomial bounds.
