# Quantum Circuit Routing Cost Functions: Generation, Evaluation & Filtering

## KEPT IDEAS (Ranked by Score)

IDEA_NAME: Harmonic Inverse Distance Priority Cost (HIDPC)
DESCRIPTION: Replace arithmetic mean aggregation over front/extended layer distances with the harmonic mean. The harmonic mean H = n / Σ(1/x_i) is dominated by its smallest values: a single nearly-executable gate (small distance) pulls the entire cost low, creating strong incentive to "complete" nearly-routable gates before attacking hard ones. The arithmetic mean in SABRE/baseline treats all gate distances uniformly; HIDPC creates asymmetric urgency that finishes easy gates first, reducing stalling.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
H_F = |F| / Σ_{g∈F} [1 / ((deps_g + 1) × d(Q1,Q2) + 1)]
H_E = |E| / Σ_{g∈E} [1 / ((deps_g + 1) × d(Q1,Q2) / layer_factor_g + 1)]
H = max_decay × (H_F + W × H_E)
(+1 inside denominator prevents division-by-zero when d=0; cost decreases monotonically as gates are resolved)
WHY_NOVEL: No existing quantum routing cost function exploits harmonic aggregation. SABRE and all lookahead variants sum or average distances arithmetically. HIDPC changes the optimization landscape from "reduce average distance" to "eliminate minimum distance," creating a fundamentally different gate completion strategy. The +1 offset ensures stability when d=0 (gate already adjacent).
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 9
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.7
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Gate Chain Length Weighted Cost (GCLWC)
DESCRIPTION: Precompute for each gate g: chain(g) = length of the longest sequence of consecutive 2-qubit gates reachable from g by following direct successor dependencies (2q-gate successors only). A gate heading a long 2q-chain represents a "routing marathon" — resolving its mapping early prevents a cascade of sequential SWAP insertions. Replace baseline's backward-looking (deps+1) weight with forward-looking chain(g), computed offline in O(|V|+|E|) by reverse topological sort over the DAG.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
chain(g) = 1 + max_{s ∈ 2q_direct_successors(g)} chain(s)   [reverse-topo DFS, precomputed]
chain(g) = 1 if g has no 2q successors
H = max_decay × (1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} chain(g) × d(Q1,Q2) + W × 1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} chain(g) × d(Q1,Q2) / layer_factor_g)
WHY_NOVEL: Baseline uses deps+1 (backward-looking: counts predecessors of current gate). Excluded CPGUC uses all-gate critical path (also forward but counts 1q gates and uses normalized ratio). GCLWC uses the longest STRICTLY 2-qubit-gate chain starting from g, which directly measures the SWAP cascade cost of delaying this gate. It is computable in O(|DAG|) offline and provides a tighter measure of routing urgency than general critical path.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 8
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.7

ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Multiplicative Layer Interaction Cost (MLIC)
DESCRIPTION: Replace the additive combination of front and extended layer costs with multiplicative coupling. In the baseline: H = F + W×E. In MLIC: H = F × (1 + W×E), where F and E are normalized layer costs. This models the compound difficulty: a hard front layer combined with a hard extended layer is multiplicatively worse, not merely additively worse. Crucially, when the front layer is nearly resolved (F → 0), the extended layer's influence naturally vanishes — preventing extended-layer noise from overriding clear front-layer decisions.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
f_dist = 1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g + 1) × d(Q1,Q2)
e_dist = (1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) × d(Q1,Q2)/layer_factor_g) if |E|>0 else 0
H = max_decay × f_dist × (1 + W × e_dist)
WHY_NOVEL: Every existing routing cost function (SABRE, qBraid, all lookahead variants) uses ADDITIVE combination H = F + W×E. MLIC introduces a MULTIPLICATIVE coupling that creates qualitatively different gradient structure: ∂H/∂e_dist = max_decay × f_dist × W (proportional to front layer difficulty), so extended layer only matters when the front layer itself is hard. This interaction term is absent from all known cost functions.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 9
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.7
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Circuit Phase Adaptive Lookahead Cost (CPALC)
DESCRIPTION: The optimal balance between front-layer urgency and extended-layer lookahead varies as the circuit progresses. Early in execution, many gates remain — extensive lookahead is valuable. Late in execution, most gates are complete — lookahead adds noise from near-empty layers. Modulate W exponentially as a function of circuit execution progress: W_eff(t) = W × exp(−α × t / T_total), where t = number of 2q-gates executed so far and T_total = total 2q-gate count. This smoothly transitions from full lookahead to front-layer-only routing.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
t = |executed 2q gates|, T = |all 2q gates in circuit|
W_eff = W × exp(−α × t / T)   [α ∈ (0,2], default α=1]
H = max_decay × (f_dist/|F| + W_eff × (e_dist/|E| if |E|>0 else 0))
[W_eff is monotone decreasing from W to ~0, guaranteeing convergence of lookahead weight]
WHY_NOVEL: All SABRE-type cost functions use a FIXED lookahead weight W throughout the entire circuit. CPALC introduces an adaptive W that decays with circuit progress, capturing the intuition that near-end routing should trust immediate distances over uncertain long-range lookahead. The exponential schedule ensures smooth, monotone adaptation with no oscillation.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 9
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.7
STATUS: KEPT

IDEA_NAME: Global Circuit Co-execution Affinity Cost (GCCAC)
DESCRIPTION: Precompute for each logical qubit pair (q1,q2): A(q1,q2) = total number of 2-qubit gates between q1 and q2 across the ENTIRE circuit (not just the current window). Use A as the routing weight instead of local dependency count. Logical qubit pairs with many global interactions benefit most from physical proximity — their affinity should drive routing priority throughout the circuit, not just when their gates happen to be in the current extended layer.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
A(q1,q2) = |{g ∈ circuit : g acts on (q1,q2)}|   [precomputed once in O(|circuit|)]
H = max_decay × (1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} A(q1_g, q2_g) × d(Q1,Q2) + W × 1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} A(q1_g, q2_g) × d(Q1,Q2) / layer_factor_g)
WHY_NOVEL: Baseline uses deps+1: a local, backward-looking count of gate predecessors. GCCAC uses A(q1,q2): a global, bidirectional count of all interactions between this qubit pair across the entire circuit. This shifts the weight from "gates that had to wait" to "qubit pairs that interact most throughout the circuit." It provides a globally consistent priority that is fully precomputed with zero per-step overhead.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 8

IDEA_NAME: Shortest Path Count Flexibility Cost (SPCFC)
DESCRIPTION: Two physical qubit pairs may share the same hop-count distance yet have vastly different routing flexibility. A pair with 8 distinct shortest paths can adapt to SWAP collisions; a pair with 1 path has no alternatives. Precompute for each physical qubit pair (P,Q): pathcount(P,Q) = number of distinct shortest paths. Scale distance by 1/√pathcount to reward routing flexibility. Gate pairs on flexible sub-graphs incur lower cost; gate pairs on brittle chains (one path) incur disproportionately higher cost.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
pathcount(P,Q) = number of distinct shortest paths from P to Q [BFS-counted, O(V×(V+E)) precomputation]
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) / sqrt(pathcount(Q1,Q2))
H = max_decay × (1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) × cost_g + W × 1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) × cost_g / layer_factor_g)
WHY_NOVEL: All existing cost functions use raw hop count. SPCFC normalizes by routing flexibility (number of shortest paths), a distinct quantity. Excluded TPNAC used 1-hop shared neighbors as a proxy for 2-SWAP meeting points. SPCFC counts ALL shortest paths globally, capturing multi-hop flexibility at any distance. A gate pair with d=3 and pathcount=1 is strictly harder to route than d=3 with pathcount=6; SPCFC encodes this distinction.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.3
STATUS: KEPT

IDEA_NAME: Physical Qubit Degree-Normalized Distance Cost (PQDNC)
DESCRIPTION: Physical qubits with higher degree (more coupling edges) offer more routing options: more potential SWAP partners, more path diversity, more flexibility under congestion. For each gate (Q1,Q2), normalize hop-count distance by the geometric mean of the two qubits' degrees. This encodes hardware topology: routing two qubits in a hub (high degree) costs less than routing the same distance in a linear chain (low degree), because the hub offers richer alternatives.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
deg(Q) = degree of physical qubit Q in coupling graph [precomputed, hardware-fixed]
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) / sqrt(deg(Q1) × deg(Q2))
H = max_decay × (1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) × cost_g + W × 1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) × cost_g / layer_factor_g)
WHY_NOVEL: Baseline and SABRE both use raw d(Q1,Q2). PQDNC introduces degree normalization, implicitly penalizing mappings that assign high-interaction logical qubits to low-degree (peripheral) physical qubits. This is purely topology-aware with zero per-step computation (degrees are precomputed constants). Different from excluded ERCRC (which uses pseudoinverse Laplacian for effective resistance) and excluded spectral methods.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 6
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 9
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.3
STATUS: KEPT

IDEA_NAME: Gate DAG Fan-out Urgency Cost (GDFUC)
DESCRIPTION: For each gate g in the DAG, precompute fanout(g) = number of direct 2-qubit successor gates. High fan-out means executing g simultaneously unblocks many downstream gates, enabling parallel execution in subsequent layers. Weighting routing cost by fanout+1 ensures that "enabler" gates (high fan-out) are resolved first, maximizing the concurrency of subsequent routing steps. This is complementary to chain-length weighting: chain prioritizes serial depth; fanout prioritizes parallel breadth.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
fanout(g) = |{s : s ∈ direct_successors(g) in DAG, s is 2-qubit gate}|   [precomputed O(|DAG|)]
H = max_decay × (1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} (fanout(g)+1) × d(Q1,Q2) + W × 1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} (fanout(g)+1) × d(Q1,Q2) / layer_factor_g)
WHY_NOVEL: Baseline uses backward-looking deps+1 (predecessor count). Excluded CPGUC uses forward-looking critical path length (longest path to output). GDFUC uses DIRECT fan-out (immediate successor count), which measures "enablement breadth" — how many gates become immediately executable upon completing g. This is a distinct urgency signal: low fan-out + long chain = bottleneck; high fan-out = parallelism enabler.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.3

IDEA_NAME: Shared Routing Path Density Cost (SRPDC)
DESCRIPTION: For each gate g in F∪E, identify its shortest routing path P*(Q1,Q2) as a set of nodes on the coupling graph. Count how many other gates in F∪E have shortest paths that intersect with P*(g). High intersection density means many gates are competing for the same routing resources (physical qubits along the path) — a spatial routing traffic jam. The cost of gate g is scaled by this path density, driving the algorithm to resolve congested gate clusters first or route them through less-contested paths.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
path(g) = node set of any shortest path from Q1 to Q2  [BFS, O(V+E) per gate]
density(g) = |{g' ∈ F∪E, g'≠g : path(g) ∩ path(g') ≠ ∅}| / (|F|+|E|-1)
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) × (1 + α × density(g))    [α ∈ [0.5, 2.0]]
H = max_decay × (1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) × cost_g + W × 1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) × cost_g / layer_factor_g)
WHY_NOVEL: Excluded CRIC/ECAC penalize CANDIDATE SWAPs that share edges with other routing paths. SRPDC evaluates the CURRENT MAPPING STATE by measuring spatial congestion of gate routing paths. It is a state-cost (evaluated once per mapping state) not a swap-cost (evaluated per candidate SWAP), making it structurally different. It acts as a congestion pressure gauge in the cost function itself.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.3
STATUS: KEPT

IDEA_NAME: Qubit Contention Penalty Cost (QCPC)
DESCRIPTION: In any routing step, multiple front-layer gates may compete for the same physical qubit's participation in a SWAP. Physical qubits have "bandwidth": only one SWAP per time step. Define contention(Q) = number of front-layer gates whose optimal routing action (greedy shortest path) requires qubit Q to move. Supplement the standard distance cost with a term penalizing SWAPs that involve high-contention physical qubits, pushing the algorithm toward lower-contention routing choices that avoid global bottlenecks.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
optimal_path(g) = BFS shortest path from Q1 to Q2  [computed per routing step]
contention(Q) = |{g ∈ F : Q ∈ optimal_path(g)}|
H(SWAP on edge (Q1,Q2)) = H_distance + β × (contention(Q1) + contention(Q2)) / (2 × |F|)
[β ∈ [0.5, 2.0]; contention values are normalized by |F| to stay scale-invariant]
WHY_NOVEL: Baseline decay parameter tracks historical SWAP usage per qubit. QCPC tracks CURRENT-STEP routing demand per qubit, measuring supply-demand imbalance in real time. Excluded CRIC measures shared edges on candidate SWAP paths; QCPC measures qubit-level demand pressure as a state property. It introduces a supply-demand economics model into routing cost functions.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.3
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Backward-Forward DAG Momentum Cost (BFDMC)
DESCRIPTION: A gate's routing urgency has two independent dimensions: how much work has already converged toward it (backward depth = deps) and how much future work depends on it (forward depth = longest path to circuit output). A gate deep in the DAG AND central to future execution is the most critical routing target. Use the product B(g) × F(g) as the urgency weight — a bilinear "momentum" measure. Gates on the critical path but with no predecessors have low product; gates deep in the circuit with many successors have high product.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
B(g) = dag_dependencies_count[g]   [already computed in baseline]
F(g) = length of longest path from g to circuit output in DAG [precomputed in O(|V|+|E|) via reverse topological sort over ALL gate types]
w(g) = (B(g) + 1) × (F(g) + 1)
H = max_decay × (1/|F| × Σ_{g∈F} w(g)/Z_F × d(Q1,Q2) + W × 1/|E| × Σ_{g∈E} w(g)/Z_E × d(Q1,Q2)/layer_factor_g)
Z_F = Σ_{g∈F} w(g), Z_E = Σ_{g∈E} w(g)   [normalizations]
WHY_NOVEL: Baseline uses only B(g)+1. Excluded CPGUC uses only F(g)/max_F (forward depth normalized). BFDMC uses the PRODUCT (B+1)×(F+1), a bilinear interaction unavailable in either alone. The product rewards gates that are simultaneously well-preceded AND well-followed — the true routing bottlenecks. Mathematically distinct from both additive combination (B+F) and single-dimension weighting.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.3
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

## ELIMINATED IDEAS

IDEA_NAME: Geometric Mean Layer Aggregation Cost (GMLAC)
DESCRIPTION: Use geometric mean (Π x_i)^(1/n) instead of arithmetic mean to aggregate gate distances within each layer. The geometric mean is less dominated by outliers than arithmetic mean and more sensitive than harmonic mean. It creates a balanced middle ground between "minimize worst distance" and "minimize all distances."
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: H_F = (Π_{g∈F} ((deps_g+1) × d(Q1,Q2) + 1))^{1/|F|}
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: H = max_decay × (H_F + W × H_E)
WHY_NOVEL: Different aggregation operator than arithmetic mean used in baseline.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 5
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Insufficiently novel — changing the mean type without changing the underlying cost structure is an incremental tweak. The geometric mean provides no principled routing-theoretic motivation, and the advantage over HIDPC (which has clear "completion first" semantics) is unclear. Redundant given HIDPC is KEPT.

IDEA_NAME: Algebraic Relaxation Distance Cost (ARDC)
DESCRIPTION: Replace hop-count distance with algebraic distance, computed via iterative label relaxation: each node initializes a random vector and iterates x_v ← average(x_{neighbors(v)}) for k steps. The L2 distance between final label vectors of Q1 and Q2 serves as a multi-scale topology-aware distance, cheaper than spectral embedding (O(k|E|) vs. O(n³) for eigenvectors).
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: d_alg(Q1,Q2) = ||x^(Q1)_final - x^(Q2)_final||_2 after k relaxation steps
WHY_NOVEL: Multi-scale distance without full eigendecomposition.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 6
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: The random initialization makes the distance stochastic — different random seeds give different routing decisions, causing non-deterministic and potentially non-convergent behavior. Fixing the seed helps but reduces novelty (similar to excluded SEDC in spirit). Risk of flat gradient in later routing stages when few gates remain.

IDEA_NAME: Topological Eccentricity Penalty Cost (TEPC)
DESCRIPTION: For each gate (Q1,Q2), penalize routing paths that pass through high-eccentricity (peripheral) physical qubits — nodes far from the graph center. eccentricity(v) = max_u d(v,u). Routing through peripheral nodes creates routing detours for future gates. Weight cost by max eccentricity along the shortest path.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: ecc_penalty(Q1,Q2) = max_{v ∈ shortest_path(Q1,Q2)} ecc(v) / diam(G)
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) × ecc_penalty(Q1,Q2)
WHY_NOVEL: Penalizes routing through graph periphery.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 6
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.0
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Low probability of outperforming SOTA. Eccentricity captures global graph structure but provides weak routing signal: the bottleneck is rarely which nodes the path passes through, but rather the distance itself. On IBM heavy-hex topologies, peripheral qubits may actually be necessary routing intermediaries with no alternatives, making this penalty counterproductive.

IDEA_NAME: Dynamic Gate Density Window Cost (DGDWC)
DESCRIPTION: Adaptively expand or contract the extended lookahead window based on local gate density. When the remaining circuit has high gate density (many gates per qubit per layer), extend the window; when sparse, shrink it. Adapts to circuit structure rather than using a fixed extended layer depth.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: window_size = k × local_density_estimate; H = max_decay × (f_dist/|F| + W × Σ_{g∈dynamic_E} d/layer_factor)
WHY_NOVEL: Dynamic lookahead window based on circuit density.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 6
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: The density estimation adds computational overhead and the adaptive window complicates convergence analysis. The CPALC idea (KEPT) already addresses adaptive lookahead more cleanly through a principled exponential decay. DGDWC is redundant with CPALC and less theoretically grounded.

IDEA_NAME: Automorphism Orbit Equivalence Cost (AOEC)
DESCRIPTION: For topologies with non-trivial automorphism groups (grids, heavy-hex rings), compute the automorphism group Aut(G). Group qubits by their orbit under Aut(G) — qubits in the same orbit are topologically equivalent. Replace distance with orbit-representative distance, and add a term penalizing mappings that assign equivalent logical qubits to different orbit classes when they share many gates.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: cost_g = d(orb(Q1), orb(Q2)) × orbital_alignment_factor(q1,q2)
WHY_NOVEL: Uses graph automorphisms to exploit hardware symmetry.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 4
EFFORT_SCORE: 4
AVERAGE_SCORE: 5.3
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Computing the full automorphism group is expensive (graph isomorphism-hard in general) and the method only applies to highly symmetric topologies. For irregular topologies (trapped ion, neutral atom arrays, future IBM devices), trivial automorphism group renders it useless. Low generalizability and implementation cost outweigh potential gains.

IDEA_NAME: Qubit Access Time Prediction Cost (QATPC)
DESCRIPTION: For each front-layer gate after a candidate SWAP, run a local mini-BFS to estimate the minimum number of additional SWAPs needed to make gate g executable, accounting for how other candidate SWAPs in the current step may help. Use this estimated access time instead of raw distance.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: access_time(g) ≈ argmin_k {∃ sequence of k SWAPs making g executable}; H = 1/|F| × Σ access_time(g)
WHY_NOVEL: Directly predicts SWAP count rather than using distance as proxy.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 4
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.0
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Computing exact minimum SWAPs needed is NP-hard in general (equivalent to routing itself). Any approximation introduces estimation error that can destabilize convergence. The mini-BFS approximation degrades to distance when other SWAPs aren't considered, losing its advantage. Convergence is not guaranteed as estimation errors can create oscillating cost signals.
