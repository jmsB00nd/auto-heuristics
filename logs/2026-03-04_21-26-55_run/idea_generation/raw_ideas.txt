Continuing from TCSC's mathematical formulation:

```
difficulty(Q1,Q2) = 1 − CC_pair(Q1,Q2) + ε,   ε = 0.01
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) * difficulty(Q1,Q2)

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / |F|
                + W * Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: Clustering coefficient has never been used as a routing cost modifier. It captures the *local topological redundancy* around each qubit — a property orthogonal to both hop-count and degree. In IBM heavy-hex (very low clustering, tree-like structure), almost all qubit pairs will have difficulty ≈ 1; in a ring or grid, clustering is higher and routing is easier. Precomputed once per topology, O(1) per gate lookup.

IDEA_NAME: Two-Hop Neighborhood Overlap Distance Cost (NODC)
DESCRIPTION: Two physical qubits that share many 2-hop neighbors are *topologically close* even if their hop-distance is 2 or 3. The Jaccard similarity of their 2-hop neighborhoods captures this structural proximity: high overlap means many common routing intermediaries exist, making routing cheap. Low overlap means the pair is topologically isolated. Scale gate distances by (1 − Jaccard), so topologically proximate pairs receive reduced cost even before their hop-count drops.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
N_2(Q) = {Q' : d(Q, Q') ≤ 2}   (closed 2-hop neighborhood)
J_2(Q1,Q2) = |N_2(Q1) ∩ N_2(Q2)| / |N_2(Q1) ∪ N_2(Q2)|   ∈ [0,1]
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) * (1 − J_2(Q1,Q2) + ε),   ε = 0.01

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / |F|
                + W * Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: The excluded TPNAC used raw 1-hop intersection count (|N(Q1)∩N(Q2)|), which is non-zero only at d=2, creating discontinuous cost landscapes. NODC uses *Jaccard of 2-hop neighborhoods*, which (1) normalizes by union size (bounded, topology-invariant), (2) remains meaningful at d=2,3,4 via 2-hop expansion, giving a smooth cost signal across all gate distances. The 2-hop extension breaks the sharp discontinuity that caused TPNAC to fail, while still capturing shared routing infrastructure.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.0

ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Successor Fan-Out Urgency Cost (SFOUC)
DESCRIPTION: A gate with many immediate successors in the circuit DAG is a *multiplier*: routing it frees up many downstream gates simultaneously. A gate with no successors (terminal) only frees itself. Weight each gate's distance by its normalized fan-out count so that gates that unlock many future operations are routed preferentially. This captures one-step downstream impact without the convergence risks of full critical-path analysis.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
fanout(g) = |{s : s is immediate successor of g in DAG}|
fanout_max = max_{g'∈F∪E} fanout(g') + 1
weight_g = 1 + fanout(g) / fanout_max   ∈ [1, 2]
cost_g = weight_g * d(Q1,Q2)

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} cost_g / |F|
                + W * Σ_{g∈E} cost_g / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: The baseline weights by *predecessor* dependency count (how many gates had to finish before g). SFOUC weights by *successor* fan-out (how many gates g will unlock). These are orthogonal: a gate can have many predecessors but few successors, or vice versa. Fan-out weighting has been used in classical scheduling but never applied to quantum SWAP routing cost functions. Unlike the excluded CPGUC (full critical path), fan-out is a single, stable, bounded integer requiring no iterative DAG traversal.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 6
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.0
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Inverse Eccentricity Distance Scaling Cost (IEDSC)
DESCRIPTION: A qubit's *eccentricity* = maximum hop-distance to any other qubit in the coupling graph. Low eccentricity = central qubit, reachable from everywhere quickly. High eccentricity = peripheral qubit, far from the graph core, requiring more SWAPs on average to reach distant partners. Scale each gate's routing cost by the average eccentricity of its physical qubit pair, normalized by the graph diameter. Peripheral qubit pairs get amplified cost, incentivizing the optimizer to bring them to better positions early.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
ecc(Q) = max_{Q'} d(Q, Q')   (eccentricity, precomputed)
diam = max_Q ecc(Q)           (graph diameter)
ecc_weight(Q1,Q2) = (ecc(Q1) + ecc(Q2)) / (2 * diam)   ∈ (0, 1]
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) * (1 + ecc_weight(Q1,Q2))   ∈ [d, 2d]

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / |F|
                + W * Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: Eccentricity is a standard graph metric but has never been applied as a routing cost modifier in quantum compilation. It captures *graph peripherality*: whether a qubit is near the routing core or stuck at the boundary. On IBM heavy-hex (linear chains at endpoints have max eccentricity), this correctly amplifies cost for boundary qubits that have few routing options. Fully precomputable once; O(1) per gate evaluation.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.0

ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Circuit Progress-Adaptive Decay Cost (CPADC)
DESCRIPTION: Early in routing, extended lookahead is crucial — the optimizer must plan for complex future interactions. Late in routing (most gates executed), the remaining circuit is small; extended layer gates are nearly irrelevant since the front layer dominates. Modulate the extended layer weight W by a decay function of circuit completion fraction p, reducing lookahead weight quadratically as the circuit completes. This concentrates routing effort where it matters most at each circuit phase.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
p = gates_executed / total_2q_gates   ∈ [0, 1]
W_adaptive(p) = W * max(0, 1 − p²)   (quadratic decay from W to 0)

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) * d(Q1,Q2) / |F|
                + W_adaptive(p) * Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) * d(Q1,Q2) / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: SABRE and all variants use a *fixed* W hyperparameter throughout routing. CPADC introduces *temporal adaptation*: the balance between immediate (front layer) and speculative (extended layer) cost shifts dynamically based on how much of the circuit remains. Near circuit completion, the extended layer becomes increasingly small and unreliable as a guide, so its weight should vanish. This matches the information-theoretic reality that lookahead is most valuable early in routing.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 6
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 9
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.0
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Shortest Path Diversity Cost (SPDC)
DESCRIPTION: Two qubits separated by d hops may have 1 or many distinct shortest paths of that length. A pair with only 1 shortest path is *routing-brittle*: any disruption (another gate needing the same intermediate qubit) forces a costly detour. A pair with 10 shortest paths has high routing flexibility. Divide each gate's distance by a log-dampened count of distinct shortest paths, so brittle pairs receive amplified cost and flexible pairs are discounted.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
SP_count(Q1,Q2) = number of distinct shortest paths of length d(Q1,Q2)
                  (precomputed via BFS-based DAG counting, O(n²) total)
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) / (1 + log(1 + SP_count(Q1,Q2)))

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / |F|
                + W * Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: Distinct from the excluded GBAC (min vertex cut) and MFRPC (max flow across all path lengths): SPDC counts only *minimum-length* paths, directly measuring how many routing solutions achieve the minimum SWAP count. This is the tightest possible measure of routing flexibility — it answers "how many ways can we route this gate optimally?" rather than "how much total flow exists?" Log-dampening prevents high-path-count pairs from getting near-zero cost. Precomputed once per topology.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 6
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.7
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Gate Dependency Pressure Diffusion Cost (GDPDC)
DESCRIPTION: A gate g that is far from executable (large d) AND has many immediate successors that are also far from executable exerts compounded routing pressure on the optimizer. Model this via a one-level diffusion: each gate's "pressure" = its own distance + a dampened sum of its successors' distances. Gates with large d AND large-d successors are doubly penalized, capturing cascade routing difficulty that a flat distance measure misses.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
α = 0.3   (diffusion coefficient, controls lookahead strength)
direct_pressure(g) = d(Q1_g, Q2_g)
successors(g) = immediate successors of g in remaining DAG

diffused_pressure(g) = direct_pressure(g)
    + α * Σ_{s ∈ successors(g)} direct_pressure(s) / (dag_depth(s) − dag_depth(g) + 1)

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} diffused_pressure(g) / |F|
                + W * Σ_{g∈E} diffused_pressure(g) / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: The baseline uses d(Q1,Q2) as a purely local metric — a gate's cost ignores whether its successors are also difficult to route. GDPDC propagates routing difficulty one level down the DAG with exponential dampening (α < 1 ensures convergence). Unlike the excluded SDAPC (full stochastic DAG propagation, unbounded), this is a single bounded sum over immediate successors only. Diffusion coefficient α ∈ (0,1) guarantees stability.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.7
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Free Qubit Opportunity Cost (FQOC)
DESCRIPTION: Physical qubits not involved in any front/extended layer gate are *routing resources* — they can serve as free intermediaries without competing with other gates' routing needs. For each gate (Q1,Q2) at distance d, count the number of *free* (unoccupied) intermediate qubits on a shortest path. Paths with many free intermediaries are cheap (no routing conflicts); paths with all-busy intermediaries are expensive. Divide distance by this opportunity count.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
busy(Q) = 1 if Q appears in any gate in F∪E, else 0
For gate (Q1,Q2) with d = d(Q1,Q2):
  free_count(Q1,Q2) = max over all shortest paths P from Q1 to Q2 of
                      |{Q ∈ P \ {Q1,Q2} : busy(Q) = 0}|
opportunity(Q1,Q2) = free_count(Q1,Q2) + 1   ≥ 1
cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) / opportunity(Q1,Q2)

H = max_decay * [ Σ_{g∈F} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / |F|
                + W * Σ_{g∈E} (deps_g+1) * cost_g / (layer_index_g * |E|) ]
```
WHY_NOVEL: No existing cost function reasons about the *occupancy state* of intermediate routing qubits. A path through all-busy qubits must interleave its SWAPs with competing gates, increasing effective routing cost. A path through free qubits can proceed unimpeded. FQOC is the first formulation to treat free qubits as a *shared routing resource* and reward paths that exploit available slack. Distinctly different from ECAC (which penalized high-load edges, now excluded).
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 6
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Gate Co-occurrence Affinity Cost (GCAC)
DESCRIPTION: For each gate (q1,q2), weight its distance by the number of other remaining gates that share at least one qubit with either q1 or q2. Qubits that co-occur in many gates are high-traffic and benefit from proximity.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: weight_g = 1 + |{g' ∈ remaining : q1∈g' or q2∈g'}| / norm; cost_g = weight_g * d(Q1,Q2)
WHY_NOVEL: Uses circuit co-occurrence structure to weight distances.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 6
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.7
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Conceptually redundant with FQIFC. Future interaction frequency (FQIFC) captures the same "hot qubit" signal more precisely by counting per-qubit gate frequency multiplicatively. GCAC counts co-occurrences additively and conflates the two qubits' individual contributions. FQIFC strictly dominates.

IDEA_NAME: Cascaded Dependency Weight Cost (CDWC)
DESCRIPTION: Weight each gate by the product of (1 + dep_count) accumulated along the chain of all predecessors, not just immediate ones. Gates at the end of long, dense dependency chains carry compounded urgency.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: cascade_weight(g) = Π_{p: ancestor of g} (1 + dep(p))^{1/depth(p)}; cost_g = cascade_weight(g) * d(Q1,Q2)
WHY_NOVEL: Captures multi-level dependency chain pressure rather than single-level.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.7
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Conceptually redundant with GDPDC. Both propagate dependency pressure through DAG levels. GDPDC uses explicit diffusion with bounded coefficient α, making it stable and principled; CDWC uses multiplicative products over all ancestors which can grow unboundedly for deep circuits, risking numerical overflow and convergence issues. GDPDC is strictly safer and more principled.

IDEA_NAME: Articulation Point Penalty Cost (APPC)
DESCRIPTION: Identify articulation points (bridge nodes whose removal disconnects the coupling graph). If a gate's shortest routing path must pass through an articulation point, there is no alternative — the route is mandatory and fragile. Add a penalty proportional to the count of articulation points on the shortest path.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: AP_count(Q1,Q2) = # articulation points on shortest path; cost_g = d(Q1,Q2) * (1 + AP_count(Q1,Q2))
WHY_NOVEL: Uses graph-theoretic vulnerability structure to identify fragile routing paths.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 6
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Most real quantum hardware topologies (IBM heavy-hex, Google Sycamore grid, Rigetti Ankaa) have zero or very few articulation points by design. The hardware is engineered to be 2-connected. APPC would be a constant scaling factor (≈1) for nearly all gates on all relevant hardware, providing no discriminative signal. Low practical improvement probability on real benchmarks.

---

IDEA_NAME: Balanced Load Entropy Cost (BLEC)
DESCRIPTION: Compute the Shannon entropy of routing distances across all front-layer gates. High entropy = routing load is spread across many different distance values (some gates close, some far). Low entropy = all gates at similar distances. Penalize high entropy as it indicates imbalanced routing that cannot be parallelized.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: p_d = (# gates in F with distance d) / |F|; H_entropy = −Σ_d p_d log(p_d); cost = baseline_cost * (1 + λ * H_entropy)
WHY_NOVEL: Uses information-theoretic load distribution of routing distances as a meta-cost.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Entropy measures the *spread* of distances but does not directly guide the optimizer toward specific swaps. High entropy is not always bad (a mix of d=1 and d=3 gates is fine if the d=1 gates are already executable). The cost signal is too indirect to reliably reduce SWAP counts. Low probability of outperforming direct distance metrics.

---

IDEA_NAME: Geometric Mean Layer Distance Cost (GMLDC)
DESCRIPTION: Replace the arithmetic mean of gate distances within each layer with the geometric mean. The geometric mean is more sensitive to outlier gates with very large distances, penalizing configurations where a single gate is extremely far.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION: H_F = (Π_{g∈F} d(Q1,Q2))^{1/|F|}; H_E = (Π_{g∈E} d(Q1,Q2)/layer_index)^{1/|E|}; H = max_decay * (H_F + W * H_E)
WHY_NOVEL: Uses geometric aggregation to amplify outlier distance penalties.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 5
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.0
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Geometric mean is a purely mathematical substitution without introducing new circuit or topology information. It does not exploit any novel signal — only changes how existing distances are aggregated. Furthermore, geometric mean is undefined when any d=0 (gate becomes adjacent), requiring special-casing that adds implementation complexity without theoretical benefit. Not fundamentally novel relative to the 13 kept ideas.
