# Quantum Circuit Routing: Novel Cost Function Ideation Report

---

## KEPT IDEAS (ranked by AVERAGE_SCORE descending)

---

IDEA_NAME: Commute Time Routing (CTR)
DESCRIPTION: Replace the hop-count distance matrix with the **commute time** between physical qubits, derived from the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of the coupling graph's Laplacian. Commute time encodes the full random-walk structure of the coupling graph: two nodes close in commute time have many short, diverse paths between them, meaning SWAPs can be routed with high flexibility. This is precomputed once and replaces `distance_matrix` entirely.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
L = D − A                          (graph Laplacian of coupling graph G_C)
L⁺ = Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse(L)
Vol(G) = Σᵢ deg(i)

CT(u,v) = Vol(G) · (L⁺ᵤᵤ + L⁺ᵥᵥ − 2L⁺ᵤᵥ)   [precomputed matrix]

w(g) = (dag_deps(g) + 1)

H_CTR = max_decay · [
    Σ_{g∈F} w(g)·CT(φ(q1_g), φ(q2_g)) / |F|
  + W · Σ_{g∈E} w(g)·CT(φ(q1_g), φ(q2_g)) / (|E|·layer_index(g))
]
```
WHY_NOVEL: Standard methods (SABRE, qlosure) use shortest-path distance, which ignores graph topology beyond single paths. Commute time naturally penalises routing through bottleneck nodes (e.g., the central qubit of a star topology) and rewards rich multi-path connectivity, an effect invisible to hop-count. The pseudoinverse L⁺ encodes the entire spectral structure of the coupling graph.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 6
AVERAGE_SCORE: 7.0
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Optimal Transport Routing Cost (OTRC)
DESCRIPTION: Cast the routing decision as an **optimal transport (Earth Mover's Distance) problem**. Define a "demand" probability measure μ over ordered physical qubit pairs — weighted by gate urgency — representing where interaction is needed. Define a "supply" measure ν over adjacent physical pairs in the coupling graph. The SWAP that minimises the Wasserstein-1 distance between the current demand and supply distributions is selected. Unlike distance averaging, this globally matches the entire interaction workload to coupling capacity.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
urgency(g) = (dag_deps(g)+1) / layer_index(g)

μ(p,q) = Σ_{g∈F∪E: φ(q1)=p,φ(q2)=q} urgency(g) / Z   (demand measure)

ν(p,q) = 1/|E_C|  ∀(p,q)∈E_C                           (supply measure, uniform on coupling edges)

W₁(μ,ν) = min_{γ∈Γ(μ,ν)} Σ_{(a,b),(c,d)} d_G(a,c)·γ((a,b),(c,d))

H_OTRC(swap) = max_decay · W₁(μ_{φ_swap}, ν)
```
WHY_NOVEL: No existing routing heuristic treats the problem as global transport matching between a demand field and a supply field. The Wasserstein distance is a proper metric on probability distributions and is sensitive to both the magnitude and spatial structure of the mismatch across the entire coupling graph, not just local pairwise distances.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 9
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 4
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.7
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Boltzmann Free Energy Routing (BFER)
DESCRIPTION: Interpret routing as a **thermodynamic system**. Define an energy E(φ) = total weighted distance and an entropy S(φ) = Shannon entropy of the gate-activity distribution across physical qubits under mapping φ. The cost is the Helmholtz free energy F = E − T·S. High entropy in the gate-activity distribution means the routing load is spread across the chip — a favourable situation. A temperature T anneals from high (entropy-dominated, exploratory) to low (energy-dominated, exploitative) as the circuit progresses.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
E(φ) = Σ_{g∈F} w(g)·D(φ(q1),φ(q2))/|F|
      + W·Σ_{g∈E} w(g)·D(φ(q1),φ(q2))/(|E|·layer_index(g))

P_p(φ) = Σ_{g: p∈{φ(q1),φ(q2)}} urgency(g) / Z   (activity prob. of physical qubit p)

S(φ) = −Σ_p P_p(φ)·log P_p(φ)

T = T₀·(1 − gates_executed / total_gates)           (linear annealing schedule)

H_BFER(swap) = max_decay · (E(φ_swap) − T·S(φ_swap))
```
WHY_NOVEL: Existing methods have no notion of load distribution entropy. The entropy term incentivises spreading future gate demand evenly across the chip, reducing congestion hotspots. The annealing schedule transitions from global exploration (tolerating higher energy if entropy is large) to local exploitation as circuits near completion — a dynamic behaviour absent from all current SOTA heuristics.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 6
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.7
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Causal Cone Depth-Weighted Priority (CCDWP)
DESCRIPTION: Weight each gate not by its simple dependency count, but by the **size of its full causal cone** — the number of all future gates that transitively depend on it in the DAG. A gate at the root of a large causal tree blocks exponentially more future gates if delayed. Additionally, weighting is normalised by remaining circuit depth, giving a "blocking pressure" score that decays for gates far from the current frontier.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
causal_size(g) = |{g' ∈ DAG | g is a transitive ancestor of g'}|  (precomputed via BFS)

depth_remaining(g) = total_circuit_depth − topological_depth(g) + 1

blocking_pressure(g) = causal_size(g) / depth_remaining(g)

H_CCDWP(swap) = max_decay · [
    Σ_{g∈F} blocking_pressure(g)·D(φ'(q1),φ'(q2)) / |F|
  + W·Σ_{g∈E} blocking_pressure(g)·D(φ'(q1),φ'(q2)) / (|E|·layer_index(g))
]
```
WHY_NOVEL: The baseline uses `dag_dependencies_count` (a local metric counting only immediate predecessors). Causal cone size is a global metric reflecting the total future impact of a gate's execution. A gate with 1 dependency but 200 descendants is treated the same as a gate with 1 dependency and 1 descendant in SABRE — CCDWP corrects this fundamental blindspot.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 5
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 7
EFFORT_SCORE: 8
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.7
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Spectral Alignment Cost (SAC)
DESCRIPTION: Compute the **spectral (Laplacian) embeddings** of both the remaining interaction graph (logical qubits, edges weighted by urgency) and the coupling graph (physical qubits). Project both into a shared k-dimensional Euclidean space and solve the orthogonal Procrustes problem to find the optimal rotation aligning the two embeddings under the current qubit mapping. The residual Frobenius norm after alignment is the routing cost — measuring how well the logical interaction topology can be "fit" onto the physical coupling topology.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
L_I = urgency-weighted Laplacian of remaining interaction graph
L_C = Laplacian of coupling graph G_C

Φ_I = [v₂,...,v_{k+1}] ∈ ℝ^{|Q|×k}   (Fiedler + k leading eigenvectors of L_I)
Φ_C = [u₂,...,u_{k+1}] ∈ ℝ^{|P|×k}   (k leading eigenvectors of L_C)

Procrustes: R* = argmin_{R:RᵀR=I} ||Φ_I − Φ_C[π]·R||_F
           solved via SVD of Φ_Cᵀ[π]·Φ_I = UΣVᵀ → R* = VUᵀ

H_SAC(swap) = max_decay · ||Φ_I − Φ_C[π_swap]·R*||_F
```
WHY_NOVEL: Spectral embeddings encode global topological similarity between graphs, capturing motifs, communities, and structural holes. No SOTA router uses spectral graph theory. This cost measures structural mismatch at the topology level, not just individual qubit distances — a SWAP that improves local distances but worsens global structural alignment would be penalised.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 5
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Hyperbolic Embedding Cost (HEC)
DESCRIPTION: Embed the coupling graph into the **Poincaré disk** (hyperbolic space H²) using Riemannian optimisation. Hyperbolic geometry is uniquely suited to graphs with hierarchical or tree-like structure (which many quantum hardware topologies approximate). Distances in the Poincaré disk between embedded physical qubits replace Euclidean/hop-count distances in the cost function. The metric naturally compresses long, bottleneck-prone paths and expands well-connected neighbourhoods.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
Poincaré embedding: x_p ∈ 𝔻² for each physical qubit p
    minimise Σ_{(u,v)∈E_C} (d_H(x_u,x_v) − d_G(u,v))²
    where d_H(x,y) = 2·arctanh(||−x⊕y||)
    and ⊕ is Möbius addition: −x⊕y = ((1+2⟨x,y⟩+||y||²)x+(1+||x||²)y)/(1+2⟨x,y⟩+||x||²||y||²)

H_HEC(swap) = max_decay · [
    Σ_{g∈F} w(g)·d_H(x_{φ'(q1)}, x_{φ'(q2)}) / |F|
  + W·Σ_{g∈E} w(g)·d_H(x_{φ'(q1)}, x_{φ'(q2)}) / (|E|·layer_index(g))
]
```
WHY_NOVEL: All existing routers use distances on the flat coupling graph. Hyperbolic geometry isometrically embeds trees and hierarchical graphs, meaning hub-and-spoke hardware topologies (heavy-hex, falcon, eagle) are more faithfully represented. SWAPs through degree-2 "bridge" nodes incur larger hyperbolic penalties than Euclidean methods detect.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 8
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 5
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Graph Frustration Routing (GFR)
DESCRIPTION: Inspired by **frustrated spin systems** in statistical physics: a logical qubit pair (a,b) is "frustrated" if their physical images φ(a), φ(b) share few common neighbours in the coupling graph. Low shared neighbourhood means there are few alternate SWAP paths, so the routing is rigid and expensive. The cost multiplies standard distance by a local frustration factor, heavily penalising SWAPs that leave pairs in "routing dead ends."
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
N(p) = set of neighbours of physical qubit p in G_C

local_frustration(a,b|φ) = 1 − |N(φ(a)) ∩ N(φ(b))| / min(deg(φ(a)), deg(φ(b)))
∈ [0,1], where 0 = fully connected neighbourhood, 1 = no shared neighbours

w(g) = (dag_deps(g)+1)

H_GFR(swap) = max_decay · [
    Σ_{g∈F} w(g)·D(φ'(q1),φ'(q2))·(1+λ·f(q1,q2|φ')) / |F|
  + W·Σ_{g∈E} w(g)·D(φ'(q1),φ'(q2))·(1+λ·f(q1,q2|φ')) / (|E|·layer_index(g))
]
where λ ∈ [0,1] is a frustration weight hyperparameter
```
WHY_NOVEL: SABRE and qlosure are purely distance-minimising — they do not consider the topological flexibility of the routing path. Two pairs at distance 2 may have vastly different SWAP flexibility: one pair has 4 alternative paths, another has exactly 1 bottleneck path. GFR captures this structural rigidity using neighbourhood intersection, a metric borrowed from frustrated magnets / social network analysis.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 6
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

IDEA_NAME: Interaction Entropy Regularisation (IGER)
DESCRIPTION: Add an **entropy regularisation term** that rewards mappings where the total routing mismatch (weighted distance) is spread *uniformly* across all physical qubits, rather than concentrated on a few. Concentration creates congestion hotspots; entropy maximisation encourages load balancing. The regulariser is the Shannon entropy of the per-physical-qubit mismatch distribution.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
base_dist(φ') = Σ_{g∈F} w(g)·D(φ'(q1),φ'(q2))/|F|
              + W·Σ_{g∈E} w(g)·D(φ'(q1),φ'(q2))/(|E|·layer_index(g))

mismatch_p(φ') = Σ_{g: φ'(q1)=p or φ'(q2)=p} w(g)·D(φ'(q1),φ'(q2))

P_p(φ') = mismatch_p / Σ_q mismatch_q    (normalised mismatch distribution)

H_entropy(φ') = −Σ_p P_p·log P_p

H_IGER(swap) = max_decay · (base_dist(φ') − λ·H_entropy(φ'))

(minimising H_IGER → minimise distance AND maximise load entropy)
```
WHY_NOVEL: All existing heuristics are agnostic to the spatial distribution of routing load. IGER introduces a second objective: mismatch diversity. A mapping where two distant pairs dominate all the distance cost is worse than one where many pairs each have small distances, even if the total distance is equal. The entropy term creates implicit load balancing without explicit qubit scheduling.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 7
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 7
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.3
STATUS: KEPT
ELIMINATION_REASON: N/A

## ELIMINATED IDEAS

---

IDEA_NAME: Quantum Walk Interference Cost (QWIC)
DESCRIPTION: Model SWAP propagation as a **continuous-time quantum walk** on the coupling graph. For each candidate SWAP, compute the quantum walk amplitude overlap between the position distributions of two interacting logical qubits at time t = π/4 (quarter-period). High overlap means they can "meet" with high quantum probability, implying fewer SWAPs needed.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
H_QW = adjacency matrix A of G_C
U(t) = exp(−i·H_QW·t)   (quantum walk unitary)

ψ_p(0) = e_p   (localised at physical qubit p)
ψ_p(t) = U(t)·ψ_p(0)

overlap(p,q,t) = |⟨ψ_p(t)|ψ_q(t)⟩|²

H_QWIC(swap) = max_decay · Σ_{g∈F} w(g)·(1 − overlap(φ'(q1),φ'(q2), t*)) / |F|
```
WHY_NOVEL: Uses genuine quantum mechanical propagation (not classical random walk) to define routing cost. Quantum interference effects between paths are fundamentally different from classical commute time.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 9
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 4
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.0
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Requires matrix exponentiation (O(n³)) per SWAP candidate per routing step. Unlike commute time (one-time precomputation), QWIC recomputes dynamically. Unclear theoretical advantage over CTR (classical commute time) for a classically-optimised problem. Computational overhead eliminates practical scalability.

IDEA_NAME: Tensor Contraction Order Cost (TCOC)
DESCRIPTION: Model the remaining circuit as a **tensor network** and use treewidth of the circuit's interaction hypergraph as a proxy for routing difficulty. A SWAP that reduces treewidth is preferred, as it decreases the complexity of all future entanglement operations.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
TN = tensor network of remaining circuit (each gate = tensor, qubits = indices)
G_TN = line graph of TN (vertices = tensors, edges = shared indices)

tw(G) = treewidth of G (NP-hard, approx. via min-fill heuristic)

ΔTW(swap) = tw(G_TN after swap applied) − tw(G_TN current)

H_TCOC(swap) = max_decay · (base_distance + α·ΔTW(swap))
```
WHY_NOVEL: Treewidth directly controls the classical simulation complexity of the circuit — and minimising it minimises the difficulty of all future routing decisions simultaneously.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 9
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 3
AVERAGE_SCORE: 6.0
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Treewidth computation is NP-hard. Even min-fill approximations are O(n²·k) where k is treewidth. Integrating this into the inner routing loop (evaluated for every candidate SWAP at every routing step) is computationally infeasible for circuits beyond ~20 qubits. Not scalable.

IDEA_NAME: Persistent Topological Routing (PTR)
DESCRIPTION: Apply **Topological Data Analysis (TDA)**: build Vietoris-Rips filtrations on both the interaction graph (with urgency as metric) and the coupling graph (with hop-count as metric). Use the Wasserstein distance between their persistence diagrams (H₀ and H₁ barcode features) as the routing cost — penalising topological mismatch between the logical and physical graph structures.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
PD_I = persistence diagram of VR filtration on interaction graph
PD_C = persistence diagram of VR filtration on coupling graph under mapping φ'

W_p(PD_I, PD_C) = inf_{γ: bijection} (Σ_{(b,d)∈PD_I} ||γ(b,d)−(b,d)||_∞^p)^{1/p}

H_PTR(swap) = max_decay · (W₂(PD_I^{H₀}, PD_C^{H₀}) + W₂(PD_I^{H₁}, PD_C^{H₁}))
```
WHY_NOVEL: First application of persistent homology to quantum routing. Captures global topological features (connected components, cycles) of the interaction structure that all distance-based heuristics completely ignore.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 10
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 5
EFFORT_SCORE: 2
AVERAGE_SCORE: 5.7
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Requires TDA library integration (Ripser, Gudhi), Vietoris-Rips complex construction, and persistence diagram distance computation — all inside the hot routing loop. Computational complexity is O(2^n) in the worst case. The theoretical benefit of topological features over spectral alignment (SAC) which captures similar global structure is unproven. Not feasible for production use.

IDEA_NAME: Flow-Capacity Routing (FCR)
DESCRIPTION: Model the coupling graph as a **unit-capacity flow network**. For each gate in the front layer, the routing cost is inversely proportional to the maximum flow (min-cut) between the two physical qubits hosting the logical operands. Low max-flow = bottleneck path = high cost.
MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION:
```
MaxFlow(p,q) = max s-t flow in G_C with unit capacities on all edges, s=p, t=q

H_FCR(swap) = max_decay · Σ_{g∈F} w(g)·(1/MaxFlow(φ'(q1),φ'(q2))) / |F|
            + W·Σ_{g∈E} w(g)·(1/MaxFlow(φ'(q1),φ'(q2))) / (|E|·layer_index(g))
```
WHY_NOVEL: Uses network flow theory to quantify path redundancy; standard methods use only shortest-path distance.
NOVELTY_SCORE: 6
PROBABILITY_SCORE: 6
EFFORT_SCORE: 5
AVERAGE_SCORE: 5.7
STATUS: ELIMINATED
ELIMINATION_REASON: Redundant with the stronger CTR idea. Commute time (pseudoinverse Laplacian) strictly generalises max-flow bottleneck intuition: it is a smooth, differentiable function of all paths simultaneously, precomputable in O(n³) once. Max-flow requires an O(n·m) Ford-Fulkerson call *per pair per SWAP candidate*, at worse total complexity. CTR dominates FCR on both mathematical richness and computational efficiency.
